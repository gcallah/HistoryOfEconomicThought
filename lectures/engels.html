1 Engels

  • The condition of the working class in England

2 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • The home manufacturer was “a bad farmer” and inefficient.
  • Nevertheless, he was better off than the industrial worker.

3 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • Children grew up in “fresh country air.”
  • Never worked more than a few hours a day.
    • Child labour was always present in the past.

4 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • They were “robbed of their trade by the introduction of machinery.”
  • The “squire” (the lord) was their “natural” superior, and they looked to him for answers.

5 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • Engels feels this class lived “without mental activity.”
  • “Intellectually, they were dead.”
  • Does Engels merely equate “not revolutionary” with “intellectually dead”?
  • They “were not human beings.”

6 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • The class of farming weavers disappeared.
  • They now lived “wholly upon wages.”
  • Spinning and weaving began the division of industrial labour.
  • When forced to compete with men working on a “sounder basis,” tey had to become wage labourers.

7 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • Result:
  • Fall in the price of manufactured commodities.
  • Prosperity of manufacturers.
  • Multiplication of proleteriat.
  • No security or property for working class.

8 Engels: The Working Class in England

  • In the early 1770s, England imported 5,000,000 pounds of raw cotton per year.
  • By 1844, that figure was 600,000,000 million pounds.
  • Population of towns rose by ten-fold.
  • No precedent in human history.
